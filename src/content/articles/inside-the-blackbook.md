Most "horses to follow" lists are static and aspirational. A trainer or a pundit names twenty horses at the start of the season, the article goes up, and that's the last anyone touches the list. Half the entries get hurt, retire, or move yards. The other half run sporadically and a couple win at big prices, which is what gets remembered.

Our Blackbook is different. It's a working document with three rules.

## The first rule: we add a horse only after we've flagged it twice

A single eye-catching run isn't enough. To make the Blackbook, a horse has to have shown up on our internal radar in at least two separate runs — typically a strong defies-rating run paired with a market signal, or back-to-back runs that suggest the trainer has it ready for a specific assignment.

This is not a methodology we describe in detail in public.
